# UX Design

Audit the UI design in `theme.md` and recommend improvements that enhance the project's design and user experience. Use this skill only after `theme.md` exists.

## UX Principles

Use the following principles to identify why a product may feel difficult or unappealing to use and guide improvements.

### 1. Provide Smart Defaults

- **Avoid:** Filling the screen with generic information, excessive text, and too many options, as many generic AI apps do.
- **Prefer:** Provide smart defaults for features, options, and components. Make the most likely choice clear through the design itself: "This is the option you will probably use most."

### 2. Use the Goal-Gradient Effect to Encourage Progress

- **Avoid:** Starting with a progress bar at 0%, which can emphasize how much work remains and discourage users.
- **Prefer:** Show an initial status of 10% or 20% to give users a sense of early progress and motivate them to continue.

### 3. Show Core Value Before Asking for Commitment

For SaaS products:

- **Avoid:** Asking users to pay, sign in, or subscribe before showing the result they came for.
- **Prefer:** Let users experience the product's core value first. Then introduce sign-in or a subscription when they want a more detailed analysis.

### 4. Use the IKEA Effect Through Personalization

- **Avoid:** Building everything for users without giving them a role in shaping their experience.
- **Prefer:** Let users personalize their profile during onboarding through background colors, avatar colors, tags, and other customizable elements. Favor words such as "personalize," "customize," and "build your own" over "create" or "set up." Consider how Duolingo approaches onboarding.

### 5. Learn from Familiar, Trusted Design Patterns

- **Avoid:** Reinventing the wheel for every design decision.
- **Prefer:** Study and adopt patterns from successful products that users already know. Familiar patterns improve consistency and reduce cognitive load. Examples include Amazon's search bar, Netflix's horizontal navigation, and Pinterest's card layout.

### 6. Communicate What Users Could Lose

- **Avoid:** Only pitching the product's features and benefits.
- **Prefer:** Clearly communicate the potential loss or missed opportunity if users do not engage with the product. Use this to create a sense of urgency and motivate action.

### 7. Use Price Contrast and Anchoring

- **Avoid:** Presenting a price as a single, standalone number.
- **Prefer:** Use the anchoring effect: show a larger number first, followed by a smaller monthly price beneath it.

### 8. Feedback and user cases.
- Always care about app's feedback to user's interactions, advoid making them confused
- Must have all CRUD, avoid missing Delete features
